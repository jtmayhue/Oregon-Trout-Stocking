Summer steelhead on the Siletz. Photo by Justin Robert Jahn NW Fishing September 3, 2026 Best bets for weekend fishing: Trout season is open for Northwest Zone streams. See the regulation booklet for limits, open areas, and bait restrictions. Cutthroat trout are present in most areas open to angling and provide a good fishery in the rivers and streams and will continue to improve from now through the end of the season (October 31). Sea-run cutthroat are dispersed throughout the fisheries. Fishing has been good recently. Nehalem Bay has fall Chinook in the bay and tidewater. Fishing has been fair and consistent the past week with multiple fish being caught daily. The Bag limit is one wild adult Chinook per day and five for the season (July 1 to Nov 30) as part of the overall daily bag limit. A few hatchery coho have been caught in the bay as well. Nestucca Bay and Tillamook Bay are both starting to produce a few Fall Chinook, it's early but there are some around and this week's rain may bring in some more. Look to higher elevation lakes that are cooling off for the best chances of success. Lost lake, South Lake, Hebo Lake, Lake Tahoe, and Town Lake are all good options for late summer trout fishing. Warm water fisheries for bass and sunfish are active and provide some good opportunity for weekend fishing. Cape Meares Lake, Coffenbury Lake, Cullaby Lake, and Vernonia pond all have decent populations of warm water species available to catch. Lake Lytle has a good largemouth bass population, but it is very weedy. The bass populations in Cape Meares and Coffenbury are healthy with decent numbers and size of fish. The population is down in Town Lake. Summer steelhead fishing on the Siletz River has been slow, but this week's rain should get some fish moving again. Multiple batches of summer steelhead were recycled to the lower river for additional angler opportunity in May and June. Recycled steelhead have a colored tag at the base of the dorsal fin. Anglers are asked to please report tagged steelhead caught in the Siletz River to caleb.w.yann@odfw.oregon.gov with the date, tag color, and general location where fish were caught to monitor recapture and harvest rates in the basin. Additional summer steelhead were also recycled to Olalla Reservoir to provide angler opportunities. The Siuslaw, Siletz and Alsea rivers are starting to produce a few fall Chinook lower in the systems. It's still early, but this week's rain should bring some more fish into the rivers. Trout angling is open in many Mid Coast streams. Sea-run and resident cutthroat can be found in many streams currently open to angling. Anglers are reminded to be aware of daily bag limits, bait restrictions and additional angling closures. Warmwater fisheries can be a great option this time of year. Siltcoos and Tahkenitch lakes can be great places to target largemouth bass, yellow perch, and bluegill. Largemouth bass are doing well at both locations. Siletz River HWY 229 remains closed at approximately milepost 14 due to a large land slide covering the road. Trout stocking Sept. 14-18: Lost Lake, Town Lake, Coffenbury Lake, Cape Meares Lake, Sunset Lake (N Coast Range) Check out the trout stocking schedule. NECANICUM RIVER: Cutthroat trout There are cutthroat trout throughout the Necanicum River fishery. Sea-run cutthroat have been in tidewater and are in the river now as well. Concentrate on deep pools with cover and use stealthy tactics for the best odds of success. Technically Fall Chinook fishing opens in the Necanicum on August 1, and there may be a few moving into tidewater, but the numbers will be low until later in the fall. The wild Chinook bag limit is one adult per day/five for the season from August 1 to November 30, as part of the overall daily bag limit. Wild Chinook jacks may be retained as per the permanent regulations (up to 5/day). See the regulation updates online at: Fall coastal salmon management for more details. The Highway 101 boat ramp south of Seaside will be closed to vehicle access until next fall to reduce dumping and illegal camping. A combination lock will be put back on the gate for angler access next fall usually around mid-October. Walk in access is still permitted through the summer. Last updated 9/1/26. NEHALEM RIVER & NORTH FORK NEHALEM: Chinook, coho, Cutthroat trout The Nehalem gets a run of "summer" Chinook salmon (an early component of the Fall run) and those fish are in the bay. Fishing has slowed a bit the past week but is still fair and consistent with multiple fish being caught daily. August is generally the peak month for the early run of Chinook. At the same time the more traditionally timed fall Chinook should be starting to show up. This is mostly a bay fishery with trolling spinners being a popular method lower in the bay and bobber fishing in the upper tidewater. Fish are dispersed from the jaws to the upper tidewater and this week's rain should push some into the river. The wild Chinook bag limit is one adult per day/five for the season from July 1 to November 30, as part of the overall daily bag limit. Wild Chinook jacks may be retained as per the permanent regulations (up to 5/day). See the regulation updates online at Fall coastal salmon management for more details. There have been a few hatchery coho caught down near the jaws, but it's probably still a little early for the Nehalem hatchery coho headed for the North Fork. There are cutthroat trout throughout the Nehalem basin and no shortage of places to fish for them. Sea-run cutthroat are dispersed throughout the fishery. Fishing has been good for them recently. Remember to check for bait restrictions before fishing. Last updated 9/1/26. NESTUCCA RIVER & LITTLE NESTUCCA RIVER: Summer steelhead, cutthroat trout, Chinook Fall Chinook are starting to show up in Nestucca Bay with a few fish being caught the past week. This week's rain could bring in more and disperse fish further up into tidewater. The wild Chinook bag limit is one adult per day/two for the season from August 1 to November 30, as part of the overall daily bag limit. Wild Chinook jacks may be retained as per the permanent regulations (up to 5/day). See the regulation updates online at: Fall coastal salmon management for more details. The Nestucca also gets a hatchery summer steelhead run, and fishing has been fair to good this summer but has slowed the last couple weeks. There are fish present throughout the fishery (up to Elk Creek), and Cedar Creek Hatchery has been recycling summer steelhead to increase angler opportunity. Some rain this week may get them more active and improve fishing. Lures, beads, and jigs are all effective techniques. There are cutthroat trout throughout the Nestucca basin. Sea-run cutthroat are dispersed throughout the fishery. Fishing should be fair to good. Last updated 9/1/26. River levels and predictions for the Nestucca are available here. SILETZ RIVER: Summer steelhead, Chinook salmon, cutthroat trout Summer steelhead are dispersed throughout the Siletz River, but fishing has been slow. The river is predicted to rise a bit from this week's rain which should get some fish moving again. Most anglers are targeting the gorge upstream of Moonshine Park. The bank access in the upper gorge offers a unique and often remote fishing experience for anglers looking to get away from crowds. Multiple batches of summer steelhead were recycled to the lower river for additional angler opportunity in May and June. Recycled steelhead will have a colored tag at the base of the dorsal fin. Anglers are asked to please report tagged steelhead caught in the Siletz River to caleb.w.yann@odfw.oregon.gov with the date, tag color, and general location fish were caught. Thank you to all the anglers who have been reporting tagged steelhead. Fall Chinook opened August 1. Anglers have reported catching a few Chinook in the bay and tidewater. The rain should bring in some fish this week. The wild Chinook bag limit is two per day/ten for the season from August 1 to December 31, as part of the overall daily bag limit. Wild Chinook jacks may be retained as per the permanent regulations (up to 5/day). See regulation updates online at Fall Coastal Salmon Management for more details. Resident and sea-run cutthroat trout are available in the Siletz River. Be sure to check your regulations before you head out for bait or stream restrictions. As a reminder, fishing is closed on the Siletz River and tributaries upstream of Siletz Falls A landslide on HWY 229 has resulted in a road closure at approximately milepost 14.5. HWY 229 will be closed for the unforeseeable future until crews are able to safely remove the debris from the roadway. As a general reminder for Anglers: Help to protect boating opportunities by maximizing distance from bank anglers and other boats. Safely row or motor around anglers and other boats and avoid motoring nearshore and in shallow areas. Last updated 9/2/26. Resident cutthroat are present throughout the upper portion of the stream. Last updated 9/1/26. TILLAMOOK BAY: Marine bottom fish, cutthroat trout, Chinook. Fall Chinook fishing opened in the Tillamook basin (Tillamook Bay, Miami R, Kilchis R, Wilson R, Trask R, and Tillamook R) on August 1, and a few are showing up. There's been a handful of Chinook caught in the bay this past week and upcoming rains could push some more in. The wild Chinook bag limit is one adult per day/five for the season from August 1 to November 30, as part of the overall daily bag limit. Wild Chinook jacks may be retained as per the permanent regulations (up to 5/day). See the regulation updates online at: Fall coastal salmon management for more details. There are still rockfish and kelp greenling available off the Tillamook Bay jetties (the North jetty provides the only bank angling this spring as the South jetty is still undergoing repairs/construction). Ling cod fishing tends to be slower this time of year but there should still be a few around. Concentrate on slack tides for the best chance of success. Sea-run cutthroat have dispersed into the rivers but there should still be a few in the tidewater. Last updated 9/1/26. TRASK RIVER: Chinook, cutthroat trout Chinook fishing is slow on the Trask. What fall Chinook are around are mostly in the bay although this week's rains could move some into the Trask tidewater. The wild Chinook bag limit is one adult per day/five for the season from August 1 to November 30, as part of the overall daily bag limit (in aggregate with Tillamook Bay, Miami R, Kilchis R, Wilson R, Trask R, and Tillamook R). Trout fishing is open and there are cutthroat trout (including sea-runs) throughout the fishery, which includes the North, South, and East forks (no bait allowed in the forks between May 22-Aug 31).
