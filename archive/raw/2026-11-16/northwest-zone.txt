Summer steelhead on the Siletz. Photo by Justin Robert Jahn NW Zone - Fall salmon fishing remained slow on the North Coast bays (Nehalem, Tillamook, and Nestucca) this week. There are still fish moving through the bays and tidewaters but with rain this week many fish are moving into the rivers. Wild coho retention is now closed on these basins, no wild coho adults may be retained as of Oct. 28. Angling for hatchery coho is still open and there are a few hatchery coho coming through Tillamook and Nehalem bays. See the regulation updates online at Fall Coastal Salmon Management for details on seasons and bag limits. Fall Chinook are in the North Coast rivers. Thus far the lower reaches of the Trask and Wilson Rivers have been the biggest producers, but there should be fish available to catch in the Nestucca, Little Nestucca, Three Rivers, Kilchis, Nehalem, and Necanicum Rivers as well. The Trask River, North Fork Nehalem River, and Lower Columbia Tributaries should have hatchery coho moving with this week's rain. The Kilchis and Miami Rivers have catch and release only fisheries for chum salmon through November 15th. The fish are in and fishing has been good. Anglers are encouraged to use good ethics and avoid disturbing actively spawning fish or redds (spawning nest in the gravel) and avoid snagging. The Nestucca and Wilson Rivers still have summer steelhead available, mostly in the upper reaches. Fall can be a good time to target these fish that have been holding most of the summer, as the dropping water temperatures and fall rains can get them more active and willing to bite. Hebo Lake and Town Lake were recently stocked with trophy trout. This was an unscheduled stocking. These are big trout that are fun to catch! Fall Chinook fishing has been slow in the Siletz, Alsea, Siuslaw, Yaquina and Salmon rivers. This much needed rain should get some fish moving throughout the basins. As a reminder, Chinook are spawning in many systems and anglers are asked to be mindful of fish on redds. Refer to Fall Coastal Salmon Management for more information on season outlines and bag limits. Wild coho retention has closed in the Siletz, Yaquin and Siuslaw rivers for the year. Siltcoos and Tahkenitch lakes are open for wild coho retention. Dedicated anglers are landing a few coho in Siltcoos Lake. The rain should raise the creeks and bring more fish into the lakes.
