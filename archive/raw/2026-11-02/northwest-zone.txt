Summer steelhead on the Siletz. Photo by Justin Robert Jahn Weekend's Best Bets NW Zone - Bring your raingear, fall salmon fishing is fair on Nehalem, Tillamook, and Nestucca bays. All basins are still producing Chinook and coho salmon. The North Coast rivers finally got some rain which will move some fish up into the rivers and may slow fishing in the bay, but there are still fresh fish coming through. Wild coho retention is now closed on these basins, no wild coho adults may be retained as of Oct. 28. Angling for hatchery coho is still open and there are hatchery coho coming through Tillamook and Nehalem bays. See the regulation updates online at Fall Coastal Salmon Management for details on seasons and bag limits. North Coast Rivers finally got some rain, and although the rivers only came up a little it was enough to move some fish in. Fall Chinook were reported moving into the lower river and fishing picked up at the hatchery hole. Fall Chinook should be moving into the lower reaches of the Necanicum, Nehalem, Kilchis, Wilson, and Nestucca Rivers as well. The Trask River, North Fork Nehalem River, and Lower Columbia Tributaries should have hatchery coho arriving with the recent rains. The Kilchis and Miami Rivers have catch and release only fisheries for chum salmon and there are probably some showing up in the lower reaches of those rivers. Anglers are encouraged to use good ethics and avoid disturbing actively spawning fish or redds (spawning nest in the gravel) and avoid snagging. The Nestucca and Wilson Rivers still have summer steelhead available, mostly in the upper reaches. Fall can be a good time to target these fish that have been holding most of the summer, as the dropping water temperatures and fall rains can get them more active and willing to bite. Many coastal streams are open for cutthroat trout fishing through Oct 31. There are resident and sea-run cutthroat available for catching. Check the regulations for open areas. Trout stocking has been completed in North Coast lakes for the year, but there are still hold-over trout available to catch in many of the lakes. Fall Chinook fishing is fair in the Siletz, Alsea, Siuslaw, Yaquina and Salmon rivers. Coho are being caught in the Siletz River. Wild coho retention in the Yaquina basin closed Oct. 25 and is scheduled to re-open Nov. 1 – 7. The Siuslaw closed for wild coho retention Oct. 13. Refer to Fall Coastal Salmon Management for more information on season outlines and bag limits. Siltcoos and Tahkenitch lakes are open for wild coho retention. Some early fish may be around, but the majority won't show up until we receive some measurable rain. Regulation Updates Please see the regulation update page .
