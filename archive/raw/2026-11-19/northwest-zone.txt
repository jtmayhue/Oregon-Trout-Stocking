Summer steelhead on the Siletz. Photo by Justin Robert Jahn NW Zone - Fall Chinook fishing has been slow to fair in the North Coast rivers. The Trask and Wilson Rivers have been the biggest producers, but there are fish being caught in the Nestucca, Little Nestucca, Three Rivers, Kilchis, Nehalem, and Necanicum Rivers as well. Fishing has really slowed down in the bays with very few fish being harvested. The Trask River, North Fork Nehalem River, and Lower Columbia Tributaries have hatchery coho salmon runs and a few fish are being caught though, overall, fishing has been slow. The Kilchis and Miami Rivers closed to catch and release fishing for chum on November 16. They are still open for fall Chinook fishing but targeting chum is prohibited. Hebo Lake and Town Lake were stocked with trophy trout a couple weeks ago. This was an unscheduled stocking. These are big trout that are fun to catch! Fall Chinook fishing has been slow in the Siletz, Alsea, Siuslaw, Yaquina and Salmon rivers. This much needed rain should get some fish moving throughout the basins. As a reminder, Chinook are spawning in many systems and anglers are asked to be mindful of fish on redds. Refer to Fall Coastal Salmon Management for more information on season outlines and bag limits. Wild coho retention has closed in the Siletz, Yaquina and Siuslaw rivers for the year. Siltcoos and Tahkenitch lakes are open for wild coho retention. Dedicated anglers are landing a few coho in Siltcoos Lake. The rain should raise the creeks and bring more fish into the lakes.
