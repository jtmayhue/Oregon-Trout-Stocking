Summer steelhead on the Siletz. Photo by Justin Robert Jahn NW Zone - Fall salmon fishing slowed on Nehalem, Tillamook, and Nestucca bays this past week following the rain. All basins are still producing a few Chinook and coho salmon. Wild coho retention is now closed on these basins, no wild coho adults may be retained as of Oct. 28. Angling for hatchery coho is still open and there are a few hatchery coho coming through Tillamook and Nehalem bays. See the regulation updates online at Fall Coastal Salmon Management for details on seasons and bag limits. Fall Chinook are entering the lower reaches of North Coast Rivers the past week with the Trask and Wilson Rivers being the most productive for anglers. The Trask River, North Fork Nehalem River, and Lower Columbia Tributaries should have hatchery coho arriving with the recent rains, but fishing has remained slow although a few fish are being caught. The Kilchis and Miami Rivers have catch and release only fisheries for chum salmon and there are probably some showing up in the lower reaches of those rivers. Anglers are encouraged to use good ethics and avoid disturbing actively spawning fish or redds (spawning nest in the gravel) and avoid snagging. The Nestucca and Wilson Rivers still have summer steelhead available, mostly in the upper reaches. Fall can be a good time to target these fish that have been holding most of the summer, as the dropping water temperatures and fall rains can get them more active and willing to bite. Trout stocking has been completed in North Coast lakes for the year, but there are still hold-over trout available to catch in many of the lakes. Fall Chinook fishing has slowed in the Siletz, Alsea, Siuslaw, Yaquina and Salmon rivers. Chinook are spawning in many systems and anglers are asked to not disturb fish on redds as they are especially susceptible during low flow conditions. Wild coho retention is open in the Siletz and Yaquina rivers through Nov 7. Refer to Fall Coastal Salmon Management for more information on season outlines and bag limits. Siltcoos and Tahkenitch lakes are open for wild coho retention. Some early fish may be around, but the majority won't show up until we receive some measurable rain.
